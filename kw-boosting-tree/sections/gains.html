The sprint has not fixed in advance the number that will say a technique gained, and will keep the numbers the literature treats as important. The report reads every paper on the same three numbers, the ones most papers on keyword boosting print side by side. The technique that acts while Whisper writes and the one that corrects its transcript are then compared on one scale.

NumberCounted onWhat it shows
WERall the words of the referencethe overall effect of a technique
B-WERthe words of the reference that are in the lexiconthe keyterms a technique recovers
U-WERthe words of the reference that are not in the lexiconthe errors a technique adds on the other words, such as an ordinary word replaced by a keyterm that was not said

The three are defined in Le et al., Interspeech 2021 (Meta), arXiv 2104.02194 (section 2). An error on a reference word counts in B-WER when that word is in the lexicon, in U-WER otherwise. The overall WER can stay flat while B-WER falls and U-WER rises, because keyterms are a small share of the words. On the LibriSpeech test sets of that paper, the words of the lexicon are about 10% of the test words.

Some papers count on keyterms only and print three other numbers: keyterm recall, the share of spoken keyterms that the transcript writes; keyterm precision, the share of keyterms written in the transcript that were spoken; and the F-score, their harmonic mean, in percent. Recall moves with B-WER, and precision falls when a keyterm is written that was not said. When a paper gives only these, the report reads it on them.

How the papers build their lexicon

Most papers that report B-WER and U-WER on public data follow the protocol of Le et al. 2021 (section 2): for each test utterance, the lexicon holds the rare words of its reference transcript, plus N distractor keyterms drawn at random from a list of rare words, with N = 100, 500, 1,000 or 2,000. A rare word there is a word outside the 5,000 most common words of the training set. The one Whisper paper of section 11 uses the same protocol with N = 1,000. A paper read at N = 500 or 1,000 therefore gives its numbers at the lexicon size of the sprint.

Two bases: vanilla Whisper, then the finetuned Whisper

Vanilla Whisper is Whisper as released, before any finetuning on mission data. The papers measure their techniques on a base model trained on general data: a vanilla Whisper, or a model of their own. None measures on a Whisper finetuned on the domain of its keyterms.

The experiments of the sprint follow the same order, each read on WER, B-WER and U-WER: a vanilla Whisper first, where the gains of the papers should show, then the finetuned Whisper. Gains are expected on the finetuned Whisper too. Its finetuning already taught it some mission words, so the keyterms left to recover there are the ones it still misses, and the numbers of the papers give the size of the effect on a base that misses more.
