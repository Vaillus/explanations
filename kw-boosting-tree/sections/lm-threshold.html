Lexicon matching sees only the transcript's text, not the audio. A common word that was said and sounds close to a keyterm looks, to it, exactly like a misspelled keyterm: the span is the same kind of string at the same kind of score. The match threshold, the score a candidate span must reach against a keyterm to be replaced by it, is the only thing that tells them apart, and it can only reject both or accept both.

Every keyterm of the lexicon that is not said in the utterance is a distractor keyterm, and each one is one more string that some ordinary word of the transcript can fall close to. Going from 100 to 1,000 keyterms does not change the spans on the left, and adds rows on the right: the chance that some keyterm sits within the threshold of an ordinary word grows with the lexicon. A keyterm that is itself an ordinary word, such as « Mistral » for a unit and the wind, scores 1 against the word and is replaced at any threshold.

The two errors show in different numbers. A keyterm recovered lowers B-WER; an ordinary word replaced raises U-WER. The threshold trades one against the other, and it is set on a set of utterances with reference transcripts and a lexicon of the mission's size, kept apart from the test utterances. Systems add guards on top of it: a threshold that depends on the keyterm's length (short keyterms are close to many words), no replacement of very frequent words, a minimum keyterm length, or a phonetic match required on top of the spelling one.
