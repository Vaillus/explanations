Whisper writes a transcript as a sequence of tokens: subword units of its fixed vocabulary of about 50,000 entries. A common word is one token; a rare word takes several. With Whisper's tokenizer, " char" is one token, " Leclerc" is four (" Le" "c" "ler" "c"), and " Kalachnikov" is four (" Kal" "ach" "nik" "ov").

At each decoding step, Whisper scores every token of its vocabulary as the next one, given the audio and the tokens written so far, and gives each a log-probability: the logarithm of its probability, zero or negative. One token is then picked to extend the transcript.

Beam search keeps several partial transcripts alive instead of one. Each is a hypothesis: the tokens written so far, with a score. The score is the cumulative log-probability, the sum of the log-probabilities of the hypothesis's tokens, so each added token lowers it. At each step beam search extends every hypothesis by its best next tokens, then keeps only the beam width best of all the extensions: dropping the rest is pruning, and a dropped hypothesis never comes back. Greedy decoding is the case of a beam width of 1, one hypothesis extended by its single best token.

The trace ends with the keyterm pruned at its second token: written in full, " Le" "c" "ler" "c" scores lower than the common words " le" " cl" at every step, so it falls out of the beam before Whisper hears its end. The techniques of this report act on that ranking.
