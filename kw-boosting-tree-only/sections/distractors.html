A false alarm is a keyterm written in the transcript though it was not said. Distractor keyterms are its main source: each one is a path of the prefix tree that beam search can be pushed down, wherever Whisper hears something close to it. The size of the boosting weight sets the trade: large enough to lift the spoken keyterms over the cut, small enough to leave the paths of distractor keyterms below it.

Three utterances on one lexicon holding « Leclerc » and « Serval ». In each, Whisper alone ranks a hypothesis made of ordinary words against the hypothesis that writes the keyterm; the boosting weight is added once per keyterm token (4 for " Le" "c" "ler" "c", 2 for " Serv" "al").

The first and third utterances flip at nearly the same weight, because « le clair » and « Leclerc » sound the same and Whisper scores them alike. No boosting weight recovers the one without writing the other: the usable range is the gap between the weight that recovers the spoken keyterms and the lowest weight at which some ordinary word flips. Each distractor keyterm adds paths that can close that gap, so a larger lexicon narrows it.

What the papers measure as the lexicon grows

From 100 to 2,000 distractor keyterms per utterance, B-WER goes from 22.2 to 22.9 against 30.6 without the tree, and U-WER stays at 7.1 to 7.2 against 7.2. On test-clean: B-WER 9.4 to 9.6 against 14.1, U-WER 2.3 against 2.4. The gain shrinks slightly with the lexicon and the other words do not suffer.

Zhao et al. 2019 (section 4.2, Table 3) gave 200 keyterms to a voice-search test set where none of them is said. With the boosting weight tuned on test sets where keyterms are said, WER on that set went from 6.9 to 12.5, an increase that biasing alone caused. With a lower boosting weight (and a change to the conditions under which biasing applies) it went to 7.3, while the sets with keyterms said kept most of their gain (for one of them, 37.0 without biasing, 7.3 at the full weight, 6.8 at the lower one).

Andrusenko et al. 2025 (section IV.C) grew a list of 200 keyterms to 20,000 with generated terms, on a model of another family than Whisper with beam search. They lowered the boosting weight from 1 to 0.7 to limit false alarms. F-score went from 81.1 to 78.4 and WER from 9.86 to 11.42, still well ahead of no biasing (F-score 44.2, WER 12.83). The run time grew by about 5%.

On Whisper itself, the one paper of section 11 gives 1,000 distractor keyterms per utterance and reports U-WER at 1.87 to 1.91 against 1.94 without the tree: at that size and with withdrawal, the other words did not suffer.
